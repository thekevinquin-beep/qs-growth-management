Investment Thesis

Buy under-managed multifamily in cities where the math still works, fix the operations first, and let disciplined asset management do the compounding.

That is the whole strategy. Everything below is how it plays out in practice — what we underwrite to, where we look, and what "hands-on" actually means on a Tuesday.

Approach

How we evaluate a deal.

Small multifamily is priced by people, not by indexes — which means the best buildings are usually the ones that have been run badly, not the ones that look best in the listing. We underwrite the building in front of us on today's income and today's rates, and treat the upside as the reward for doing the work.

01
In-place income, conservative expenses.
The deal has to cover its debt on the rent roll that exists at closing, with expenses budgeted at what a building actually costs to run — not what the seller reported.
02
Operational upside, not construction risk.
We look for buildings where the gap to market is tenant quality, leasing discipline, and expense control. Mount Royal and Westlynn were both fixed with management, not with contractors.
03
A clear path to refinance.
Every deal is underwritten to a stabilized refinance on proven numbers. That is how operational work becomes a return to the partnership — and how capital gets recycled into the next building.
Criteria

What we look for. What we walk past.

We look for
  • 15–50 unit walk-up or garden-style buildings — small enough to manage hands-on, large enough for economies of scale.
  • Established neighbourhoods with stable employment bases and rents that still clear today's borrowing costs.
  • Evidence of neglect that is operational, not structural: below-market rents, weak screening, deferred maintenance, absent ownership.
  • Sellers and partners who value certainty and a straightforward process over the last dollar.
We walk past
  • Deals that only pencil on projected rent growth or a future cap-rate compression.
  • Heavy-renovation plays where the return depends on construction budgets and timelines we don't control.
  • Markets we can't be physically present in, or partners we can't be direct with.
  • Anything that requires us to overstate what we've done to raise the money to do it.
Where We Invest

Discipline over geography.

We are not an "Edmonton fund" or a "Saskatchewan fund." We are a set of criteria — and right now, the Prairie cities are where those criteria are easiest to meet. Purchase prices relative to rents still make sense, the employment bases are diversified enough to weather a cycle, and small buildings trade between individuals rather than institutions, which keeps pricing rational.

How We Operate

Asset management as the core discipline.

Acquisition is one day. Operations are every day after. In a 20-unit building there is no management layer to hide behind — the person who signs the offer is the person who deals with the leaking suite, the late rent, and the roofing quote. We think that is an advantage, not a burden.

  1. 01

    Weekly presence

    Walking the property, talking to tenants, and catching small problems while they are still small. Deferred maintenance is the most expensive line item in multifamily, and the only cure is showing up.

  2. 02

    Tenant quality over occupancy

    A full building with the wrong tenants is worth less than a 90%-occupied building with the right ones. We hold a consistent screening standard and accept short vacancy to get there.

  3. 03

    Vendors as partners

    A small bench of trades we use repeatedly, pay promptly, and hold to a standard. It is how a 20-unit building gets the response time of a 200-unit one.

  4. 04

    Plain reporting

    Monthly actuals against budget for every investor and owner. The numbers we manage to are the numbers you see — no narrative layer in between.
